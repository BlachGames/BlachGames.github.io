OVERVIEW

The Project

Between Frequencies is a survival and exploration game built around isolation, navigation and uncertainty.

The player takes the role of a seismologist arriving in Antarctica in 1976 to investigate unexplained seismic activity.

Research bases have been abandoned, communication is unreliable and something appears to be moving through the storm.

Instead of relying on traditional objective markers, the game uses radio frequencies, audio and environmental clues to guide the player.

02

MY WORK

What I Worked On

My focus was creating gameplay systems that supported navigation, atmosphere and player feedback.

Radio Navigation

Developed a navigation mechanic where signal strength changes based on the player's direction and distance from an objective.

Gameplay Systems

Worked on player interaction, exploration, puzzles and environmental gameplay systems.

Player Feedback

Used audio, UI, environmental effects and visual feedback to communicate danger and player progress.

Atmosphere

Worked with weather effects, sound, post-processing and environmental presentation to support the horror experience.

03

TECHNICAL

Radio Navigation

One of the main mechanics replaces a traditional waypoint with a radio signal the player must interpret.

Signal quality is calculated using several factors, including how close the player is to the target and whether they are facing in the correct direction.

Two audio sources are blended together to create transitions between clear radio transmission and static. Additional filtering helps communicate whether the player is moving closer to or farther from the signal.

04

DESIGN

Navigation Without Waypoints

The goal was to make navigation itself part of the gameplay rather than simply telling the player where to go.

The radio provides information, but the player still needs to interpret that information and understand their surroundings.

This allowed exploration and uncertainty to remain important while still giving the player enough information to make progress.

05

ITERATION

Playtesting & Feedback

Playtesting showed where atmosphere was supporting the experience and where important gameplay information was becoming too difficult to understand.

Some mechanics initially relied too heavily on subtle environmental cues.

I worked on making objectives and danger states clearer through stronger audio cues, onboarding and visual feedback without removing the feeling of uncertainty.

06

REFLECTION

What I Learned

Between Frequencies taught me how closely technical systems and player experience are connected.

A navigation mechanic can work correctly from a programming perspective while still being difficult for players to understand.

Iterating on the radio system helped me think more carefully about readability, feedback and how much information a mechanic should communicate.
